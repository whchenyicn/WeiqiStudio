When you first see a full Weiqi board, it can feel huge.

A standard board has 19 lines in each direction and 361 intersections.

That is a lot of space for a beginner.

Fortunately, you do not need to start there.

Weiqi can be played on smaller boards, and smaller boards are often the best way to learn.

The most common beginner board sizes are:

- 9x9
- 13x13
- 19x19

Each board teaches something useful.

The key is choosing the right size for your current level.

## Why Does Weiqi Have Different Board Sizes?

The full game of Weiqi is traditionally played on a 19x19 board.

That is the standard size used in serious games, tournaments, and professional play.

But the rules of Weiqi work on smaller boards too.

This makes smaller boards very useful for learning.

On a smaller board, games finish faster, mistakes are easier to see, and beginners can focus on the basic rules without getting lost.

If you are still learning what Weiqi is, start with the guide to [What Is Weiqi?](/articles/what-is-weiqi) before worrying about board size too much.

## Quick Comparison

Here is a simple comparison of the three most common Weiqi board sizes:

| Board size | Number of intersections | Typical game length | Difficulty | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9x9 | 81 | 10–20 minutes | Easiest | Complete beginners, children, first games |
| 13x13 | 169 | 20–40 minutes | Medium | Beginners who know the rules and want more space |
| 19x19 | 361 | 45–90+ minutes | Hardest | Full games, tournaments, advanced study |

These times are only estimates.

Some games are faster.

Some games take longer.

The important point is that larger boards create more choices.

More choices means more depth, but also more confusion for beginners.

## 9x9 Weiqi Board

The 9x9 board is the best starting point for most beginners.

It is small enough to understand quickly, but still large enough to show the basic ideas of Weiqi.

On a 9x9 board, you can practice:

- Taking turns.
- Counting liberties.
- Capturing stones.
- Making territory.
- Ending and scoring a game.

Because the board is small, contact happens quickly.

You do not need to wait many moves before stones begin interacting.

This makes the game easier to follow.

Mistakes also become clear faster.

If a group is weak, you often notice it within a few moves.

If you give up too much territory, the result becomes obvious quickly.

That kind of feedback is valuable when you are learning.

## Advantages of 9x9

The biggest advantage of 9x9 is speed.

You can finish a game in a short session.

That means you can play many games, make many mistakes, and learn from them without spending an entire evening on one board.

9x9 is also less intimidating.

There are fewer empty intersections, fewer long-term plans, and fewer complicated opening ideas.

For beginners, this is a good thing.

You can focus on the rules instead of trying to understand the whole board at once.

## 13x13 Weiqi Board

The 13x13 board is a bridge between beginner games and full-sized games.

It gives you more room than 9x9, but it is still much easier to manage than 19x19.

After you understand the basics, 13x13 helps you begin thinking about larger ideas:

- Corners
- Sides
- Center influence
- Bigger territory
- Longer fights
- Connecting groups across distance

This board size is useful when 9x9 starts to feel too small, but 19x19 still feels overwhelming.

Many beginners skip 13x13 and move straight from 9x9 to 19x19.

That is possible.

But spending time on 13x13 can make the transition smoother.

## Advantages of 13x13

The biggest advantage of 13x13 is balance.

It still keeps games fairly short, but it introduces more strategic space.

On 9x9, the whole board can feel like one fight.

On 13x13, you begin to see different areas of the board develop at the same time.

You may have one fight on the side, one territory in the corner, and one weak group in the center.

This helps prepare you for full 19x19 games without overwhelming you immediately.

## 19x19 Weiqi Board

The 19x19 board is the standard board for Weiqi.

This is the board used in most tournaments, professional games, serious online games, and traditional study.

It offers the full depth of the game.

On a 19x19 board, there is room for:

- Opening strategy
- Large frameworks
- Multiple battles
- Invasions
- Reductions
- Territory and influence
- Long-term planning

This is where Weiqi becomes especially deep.

But that depth can be difficult for complete beginners.

If you start on 19x19 too early, the board may feel empty, confusing, and slow.

You may not know where to play.

That does not mean you are bad at Weiqi.

It only means the board is giving you too many choices at once.

## Advantages of 19x19

The biggest advantage of 19x19 is depth.

It shows the full beauty of Weiqi.

You can build large territories, attack weak groups, sacrifice stones, use influence, and make plans that unfold over many moves.

If 9x9 teaches the rules, 19x19 teaches the whole game.

This is also the board size you will eventually want to understand if you plan to study classic games, watch professional matches, or play in tournaments.

## Should Beginners Start with 9x9?

Yes.

Most beginners should start with 9x9.

This includes adults.

Some adults feel they should begin on 19x19 because it is the "real" board.

But learning on 9x9 is not childish.

It is efficient.

Strong players, teachers, and online servers often use 9x9 to introduce new players because it makes the basic rules easier to see.

If you want to [play your first game](/articles/play-your-first-game), a 9x9 board is usually the best choice.

## Should Children Learn Differently?

Children often do especially well on 9x9.

Shorter games help them stay focused.

Captures happen more often, which makes the game feel concrete and easier to understand.

For very young children, even smaller teaching boards can sometimes be useful, but 9x9 is the common beginner size.

Children can move to 13x13 or 19x19 when they are comfortable finishing games and understanding simple territory.

The goal is not to rush.

The goal is to keep learning enjoyable and clear.

## Common Learning Progression

A simple learning path looks like this:

1. Learn the basic rules.
2. Play several 9x9 games.
3. Practice counting liberties and capturing.
4. Learn how territory works.
5. Move to 13x13 when 9x9 feels comfortable.
6. Try 19x19 when you can finish games without feeling completely lost.

This progression is flexible.

Some players move faster.

Some players stay on 9x9 longer.

Both are fine.

If you are wondering how long this process usually takes, read [How Long Does It Take to Learn Weiqi?](/articles/how-long-does-it-take-to-learn-weiqi).

## Which Board Size Is Used in Tournaments?

Most official Weiqi tournaments use the 19x19 board.

This is the standard board size for serious play.

Professional games are also played on 19x19.

However, smaller-board events and teaching games can use 9x9 or 13x13.

Online servers may also offer all three sizes.

As a beginner, you do not need to worry about tournament standards immediately.

Your first goal is to understand the game.

The 19x19 board will still be there when you are ready.

## Beginner Recommendation

If you are completely new, start with 9x9.

Play enough games to understand liberties, capturing, territory, and scoring.

Then try 13x13 when you want more space.

Move to 19x19 when you feel ready for longer games and deeper strategy.

There is no prize for rushing.

The best board size is the one that helps you learn clearly.

## Quick Recap

Before moving on, remember these key ideas:

- 9x9 is best for complete beginners.
- 13x13 is a useful bridge between small and full-sized games.
- 19x19 is the standard board for tournaments and professional play.
- Adults should not feel embarrassed about starting on 9x9.
- Children often learn well on 9x9 because games are shorter.
- Most players progress from 9x9 to 13x13 to 19x19.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 9x9 real Weiqi?

Yes.

The board is smaller, but the core rules are still Weiqi.

### Should adults start on 9x9?

Yes.

Adults usually learn faster on 9x9 because the games are shorter and the basic ideas are easier to see.

### When should I move from 9x9 to 13x13?

Move to 13x13 when you can finish 9x9 games and understand basic liberties, captures, territory, and scoring.

### When should I move to 19x19?

Move to 19x19 when you want deeper games and no longer feel completely lost on smaller boards.

### What board size do tournaments use?

Most serious tournaments and professional games use the 19x19 board.
