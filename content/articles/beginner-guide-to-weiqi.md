## What Is Weiqi?

Weiqi is a strategy board game played with black and white stones.

Players take turns placing stones on the intersections of a grid.

The goal is to control more territory than your opponent by the end of the game.

At first, Weiqi looks simple.

But every move changes the whole board.

That is why many players love it.

It rewards patience, creativity, balance, and long-term thinking.

## Opening Fundamentals

The opening is the beginning of the game.

Beginners often think they need to memorize opening patterns.

You do not.

At the beginning, it is more useful to understand simple principles:

- Corners are efficient.
- Sides are useful next.
- The center becomes powerful later.
- Good opening moves help your stones work together.

## Essential Beginner Strategy

Once the rules feel familiar, strategy becomes easier to study.

Beginner strategy is not about playing like a professional.

It is about making better choices:

- Build territory.
- Protect weak groups.
- Avoid unnecessary fights.
- Use strong stones well.
- Know when to answer and when to play elsewhere.

## Life and Death

Life and death is the study of whether groups can survive.

For beginners, the most important idea is simple:

Groups usually need two real eyes to be safe.

You do not need advanced reading yet.

Start by learning how eyes, liberties, false eyes, and weak groups work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does it take to learn Weiqi?

You can learn the basic rules in one short session.

Feeling comfortable takes longer.

For a realistic timeline, read the learning timeline guide linked above.

### Should beginners start with 9x9?

Yes.

A 9x9 board is shorter, clearer, and easier to finish.

You can compare board sizes in the board-size guide linked above.

### Should I memorize joseki?

Not at first.

Beginners should learn opening principles before memorizing sequences.

Start with the first-move guide linked above.

### Should I play against AI?

You can, but do not rely on AI alone.

Real games, simple reviews, and beginner problems are more useful at first.

For tools, read the beginner apps guide linked above.

### How often should I play?

Two to four thoughtful games per week is enough for many beginners.

Consistency matters more than marathon sessions.

Read the practice-frequency guide linked above.

### What is the fastest way to improve?

Play small games, review one mistake, solve easy tsumego, and study simple concepts.

The full routine is explained in the study guide linked above.

### Do I need to know Chinese and Japanese rules?

Not deeply.

Learn the rules your app, server, teacher, or club uses.

The beginner differences are explained in the rules comparison linked above.

### Are handicap games good for beginners?

Yes.

They help players of different strengths enjoy a meaningful game.

Read the handicap games guide linked above.

### What should I learn after the beginner course?

Start learning strategy, life and death, and review habits.

Good next steps include influence, tsumego, and game review.
