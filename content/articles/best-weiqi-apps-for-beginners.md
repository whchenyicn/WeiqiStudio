Apps can make learning Weiqi much easier.

You can play a short game.

You can solve a few problems.

You can review a mistake.

You can learn the rules without setting up a physical board.

But beginners do not need every app.

The best app is the one that helps you take the next simple step.

This guide compares beginner-friendly Weiqi and Go apps without treating any one app as perfect for everyone.

## Why Apps Are Useful for Learning Weiqi

Apps are useful because they make practice convenient.

You can use them for five minutes.

You can play on a 9x9 board.

You can repeat basic problems.

You can review games without needing a teacher immediately.

For beginners, this matters because consistency is more important than long study sessions.

If you are building a study routine, read [How to Study Weiqi Effectively as a Beginner](/articles/how-to-study-weiqi-effectively).

## Different Types of Weiqi Apps

Not all apps do the same job.

Most apps fit into one of four groups:

- Playing games
- Solving tsumego
- Learning the rules
- Reviewing games with AI

Some apps combine several of these.

Others are better at one specific task.

As a beginner, it is usually better to choose one simple tool for each need instead of installing everything at once.

## 1. OGS

OGS stands for Online Go Server.

It is one of the easiest places for beginners to play Weiqi online in a browser.

### Best for

OGS is best for normal online games.

It is especially useful if you want one main place to play 9x9, 13x13, or 19x19 games.

### Strengths

- Works in a browser
- Supports different board sizes
- Good for live and slower games
- Beginner-friendly compared with many older servers
- Useful for reviewing completed games

### Possible drawbacks

Online games can still feel intimidating at first.

Ranks may also feel confusing until you have played more games.

### Beginner recommendation

Use OGS when you are ready to play real opponents.

If you want more detail, read [Best Places to Play Weiqi Online](/articles/best-online-places-to-play-weiqi).

## 2. GoQuest

GoQuest is popular for quick games.

It is especially useful for 9x9.

### Best for

GoQuest is best for fast mobile games.

It is useful when you want short practice instead of a long serious game.

### Strengths

- Quick matchmaking
- Very convenient on mobile
- Good for 9x9 practice
- Helps beginners get many complete games
- Simple enough to start quickly

### Possible drawbacks

Because games are fast, beginners may move too quickly.

Fast games can build bad habits if they are your only practice.

### Beginner recommendation

Use GoQuest for quick 9x9 games.

Do not use it as your only learning method.

After games, review one mistake.

## 3. SmartGo

SmartGo is a broad study app for Go.

It can include problems, game records, books, computer play, and study tools depending on the version and platform.

### Best for

SmartGo is best for study and reference.

It is useful if you want one app that can support several parts of learning.

### Strengths

- Good for studying games and positions
- Includes problem-solving features
- Useful for recording or reviewing games
- Offers access to Go books and game records
- Stronger as a long-term study tool

### Possible drawbacks

Because it offers many features, complete beginners may not need everything immediately.

Some content or features may require payment.

### Beginner recommendation

Use SmartGo if you want a serious study tool and are comfortable exploring.

If you only want to play your first few games, start simpler.

## 4. BadukPop

BadukPop focuses on learning, problems, and casual practice.

It can be useful for beginners who want guided practice without jumping straight into serious online games.

### Best for

BadukPop is best for learning rules, solving beginner problems, and practicing in a lighter app environment.

### Strengths

- Beginner-friendly rule learning
- Tsumego practice
- AI opponents and online features
- Easy to use in short sessions
- Good for building confidence

### Possible drawbacks

The style may feel more playful than some learners prefer.

Some features may be limited behind paid options.

### Beginner recommendation

Use BadukPop if you want a gentle way to learn and practice.

It can be especially useful before or alongside real online games.

## 5. Go Magic

Go Magic is more of a learning platform than a simple playing app.

It focuses on structured lessons, exercises, and guided study.

### Best for

Go Magic is best for learning rules and concepts through organized lessons.

### Strengths

- Structured courses
- Beginner-focused lessons
- Practical exercises
- Clear learning path
- Useful when random study feels confusing

### Possible drawbacks

Some courses and features are paid.

It is not mainly a quick matchmaking app.

### Beginner recommendation

Use Go Magic if you want guided lessons and explanations.

It pairs well with a playing app like OGS or GoQuest.

## 6. AI Sensei

AI Sensei is designed for reviewing Go games with AI.

It can help players find mistakes and study better alternatives.

### Best for

AI Sensei is best for game review.

It is more useful after you understand the basic rules and have a few games to review.

### Strengths

- Helps identify big mistakes
- Works in a browser
- Useful for reviewing uploaded games
- Can show alternative moves
- Helpful for focused study after playing

### Possible drawbacks

AI review can overwhelm complete beginners.

If you do not yet understand liberties, territory, and basic life and death, AI suggestions may feel confusing.

Some stronger analysis features may require payment.

### Beginner recommendation

Wait until you have played several games before using AI Sensei seriously.

Start with simple self-review first.

Then use AI review to check one or two big mistakes.

For a beginner review process, read [How to Review Your Weiqi Games and Improve Faster](/articles/how-to-review-your-weiqi-games).

## Which App Should You Start With?

The best choice depends on your current stage.

If you are a complete beginner, use this simple progression:

1. Learn the rules.
2. Play 9x9.
3. Solve tsumego.
4. Review games.

This order keeps learning practical.

You do not need advanced AI review before you understand the board.

You do not need difficult tsumego before you know eyes and liberties.

You do not need long 19x19 games before you can finish a 9x9 game calmly.

## A Simple Beginner App Setup

Here is a simple setup:

- Use Go Magic or BadukPop to learn the rules.
- Use OGS for normal online games.
- Use GoQuest for quick 9x9 practice.
- Use BadukPop or SmartGo for easy tsumego.
- Use AI Sensei later for review.

This is only a suggestion.

Choose the tools that make you more likely to practice calmly and consistently.

## How Apps Fit Into a Study Routine

Apps should support your learning routine.

They should not replace thinking.

A good weekly rhythm might look like this:

- Play two or three short games.
- Review one mistake after each game.
- Solve a few easy tsumego.
- Read one short lesson.

## Common Mistakes When Using Apps

### Installing too many apps

Too many tools can become distracting.

Start with one or two.

### Playing only fast games

Fast games are useful, but they can make you rush.

Play some slower games too.

### Trusting AI without understanding

AI may show a better move, but beginners need simple explanations.

If an AI suggestion is confusing, focus on one obvious mistake instead.

### Solving problems that are too hard

Easy tsumego is better than frustrating tsumego.

Build confidence first.

### Never reviewing games

Playing many games without review can repeat the same mistakes.

Use apps to help you learn, not only to play more.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the best Weiqi app for beginners?

There is no single best app for everyone.

Many beginners can start with a learning app, then use OGS or GoQuest for games.

### What is the best Go app for playing online?

OGS is a strong default choice for browser-based games.

GoQuest is useful for quick mobile games, especially 9x9.

### Should beginners use AI review apps?

Not immediately.

AI review is more useful after you understand the rules and can review simple mistakes yourself.

### Are paid Go apps necessary?

No.

Paid tools can be useful, but beginners can learn a lot with free lessons, free games, and simple practice.

### Should I learn Weiqi from an app only?

Apps are helpful, but you will improve faster if you also play real games, review mistakes, and study basic concepts.
