Beginners often hear that Weiqi has different rulesets.

Chinese rules.

Japanese rules.

Korean rules.

AGA rules.

This can sound confusing.

But the good news is simple:

The game is still the same game.

You still place stones.

You still capture by removing liberties.

You still surround territory.

You still try to finish with the higher score.

For beginners, the differences between Chinese and Japanese rules matter much less than learning how the game works.

## Why Are There Different Rulesets?

Weiqi is a very old game.

It spread across different countries and cultures over many centuries.

China, Japan, Korea, and other Go communities developed their own formal rule traditions.

Most of the basic rules stayed the same.

But the details of scoring, passing, ko, handicap, and endgame disputes developed slightly differently.

That is why modern players may talk about Chinese rules, Japanese rules, Korean rules, AGA rules, and other systems.

For beginners, the most important difference is scoring.

## A Brief History

Weiqi began in China.

Over time, the game spread to Korea and Japan.

Each country built its own playing culture, teaching tradition, and tournament rules.

Japanese Go became especially influential in many English-language books and clubs.

That is why many English resources use Japanese terms and often explain Japanese-style scoring.

Chinese rules are also widely used, especially in Chinese Weiqi and many modern online or AI-friendly contexts.

You do not need to memorize the history.

Just remember that different rulesets grew from different Go cultures.

## The Biggest Practical Difference

The biggest beginner difference is:

Chinese rules usually use area scoring.

Japanese rules usually use territory scoring.

Both systems usually lead to the same winner.

They simply count the board in different ways.

If you already understand [scoring in Weiqi](/articles/scoring-in-weiqi), this difference becomes much easier.

## Area Scoring

Chinese rules use area scoring.

Under area scoring, your score is based on:

- The empty territory you surround
- Your living stones on the board

In simple terms:

Territory plus living stones.

This makes Chinese-style scoring feel very direct.

You are counting the area your stones control.

Because stones on the board count, filling neutral points near the end usually does not hurt your score in the same way beginners may fear.

## Territory Scoring

Japanese rules use territory scoring.

Under territory scoring, your score is based on:

- The empty territory you surround
- Captured stones or prisoners

In simple terms:

Territory plus captures.

This is why beginners often hear:

Do not fill your own territory.

If you place unnecessary stones inside your own secure territory under Japanese-style scoring, you may reduce your own points.

This sounds scary at first, but it becomes natural with practice.

If territory still feels unclear, review [What Is Territory in Weiqi?](/articles/territory-in-weiqi).

## Chinese Rules vs Japanese Rules

Here is a beginner-friendly comparison:

| Topic | Chinese Rules | Japanese Rules |
|-------|---------------|----------------|
| Main scoring style | Area scoring | Territory scoring |
| What you count | Territory plus living stones | Territory plus captured stones |
| Beginner feeling | Often easier to understand as board control | Often familiar from books and traditional teaching |
| Filling neutral points | Usually less punishing | More important to avoid filling your own territory unnecessarily |
| Online use | Common on many servers and with bots | Common on many servers, clubs, and tournaments |
| Strategy difference | Very small for beginners | Very small for beginners |

This table simplifies many details.

That is intentional.

Complete beginners do not need edge cases yet.

## Which Rules Should Beginners Learn?

Beginners can start with either ruleset.

If your teacher, club, app, or online server uses Chinese rules, learn Chinese rules.

If your teacher, club, app, or online server uses Japanese rules, learn Japanese rules.

Do not spend too much time worrying about which one is perfect.

The most important beginner skills are the same:

- Count liberties
- Capture stones
- Make eyes
- Build territory
- Know when the game is finished
- Review your mistakes

If you are still new, focus on [playing your first game](/articles/play-your-first-game).

The ruleset can be adjusted later.

## Do the Different Rules Change Strategy?

At a beginner level, not much.

The same moves are usually good.

The same weak groups are still weak.

The same territory still matters.

The same bad habits still cause problems.

There are small endgame and scoring differences, but they rarely matter when you are learning the basics.

For most beginner games, the winner will be the same under Chinese or Japanese rules.

The larger strategic ideas do not change.

You should still try to control more of the board than your opponent.

## Which Rules Are Used on Online Servers?

Online servers vary.

The safest habit is to check the game settings before you start.

Common examples:

- OGS supports several rulesets, including Japanese and Chinese rules.
- KGS supports multiple rulesets, including Japanese, Chinese, AGA, and New Zealand rules.
- Pandanet / IGS is often associated with Japanese-style tournament play.
- Some apps and bots use Chinese or area-style scoring because it can be easier to automate.

Do not worry if this feels like a lot.

Most servers count the score for you.

You mainly need to know which ruleset your game is using and avoid making unnecessary moves in your own territory.

If you are choosing where to play, read [Best Places to Play Weiqi Online](/articles/best-online-places-to-play-weiqi).

## Does Learning One Ruleset Make the Other Difficult?

No.

Learning one ruleset does not make the other difficult.

Once you understand the board, the difference is mostly about scoring details.

If you first learn Chinese rules, Japanese territory scoring may feel a little more careful at the end.

If you first learn Japanese rules, Chinese area scoring may feel a little more direct.

But the core game is unchanged.

You can switch later without starting over.

## Why Beginners Should Not Worry Too Much

Beginners have more important things to learn first.

For example:

- What is a liberty?
- When is a group captured?
- What is territory?
- When should both players pass?
- Which groups are alive or dead?

These ideas matter in every ruleset.

If those basics are clear, the scoring differences become much easier.

If they are not clear, reading rule debates will only make the game feel harder than it is.

## Common Beginner Confusions

### Thinking the games are different

Chinese rules and Japanese rules do not create different games.

They are different ways to formalize and score the same game.

### Thinking captures always decide the winner

Captures matter, especially in Japanese scoring.

But the goal is still to finish with the higher score.

Territory and board control remain central.

### Overthinking endgame edge cases

Advanced rules discussions can become complicated.

Beginners do not need to understand rare disputes yet.

### Ignoring the server settings

When playing online, check the rules before the game begins.

If the server scores automatically, still try to understand the basic method.

## Key Takeaways

Before moving on, remember these ideas:

- Chinese and Japanese rules are different rule traditions for the same game.
- The biggest beginner difference is scoring.
- Chinese rules usually use area scoring.
- Japanese rules usually use territory scoring.
- Beginners can start with either ruleset.
- Most beginner strategy does not change.
- Online servers often count for you, but you should still check the rules setting.
- Learning one ruleset does not make the other difficult.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between Chinese and Japanese rules in Weiqi?

The main beginner difference is scoring.

Chinese rules usually count territory plus living stones, while Japanese rules usually count territory plus captured stones.

### What is area scoring?

Area scoring counts the area you control, including your surrounded territory and your living stones on the board.

### What is territory scoring?

Territory scoring counts the empty territory you control, plus captured stones or prisoners.

### Which rules should beginners learn first?

Learn whichever rules your app, teacher, club, or online server uses.

The basic game is the same.

### Do Chinese and Japanese rules usually produce different winners?

Usually no.

For most beginner games, the winner will be the same.
