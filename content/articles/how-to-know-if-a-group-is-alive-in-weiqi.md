One of the most important beginner questions in Weiqi is:

Is this group alive?

At first, this can feel difficult.

Your stones may look safe.

They may have many stones.

They may even surround some empty space.

Then suddenly, your opponent attacks and the whole group disappears.

This happens to every beginner.

Learning how to recognize living groups is one of the first steps toward better strategy.

## What Does an Alive Group Mean?

An alive group is a group of stones that cannot be captured if both players play correctly.

The group may be surrounded.

It may not have much territory.

It may look small.

But if your opponent cannot remove it from the board, it is alive.

In beginner Weiqi, life usually depends on two things:

- Liberties
- Eyes

If you need a refresher, start with [liberties](/articles/liberties-in-weiqi) and [eyes](/articles/eyes-in-weiqi).

## Why Beginners Lose Groups Unexpectedly

Beginners often lose groups because they look only at the outside shape.

A group may look large, but still be weak.

It may have many stones, but few liberties.

It may surround one empty point, but not have enough eye space.

It may be cut off from friendly stones.

This is why a group can feel safe one moment and suddenly become captured later.

The problem is not that the rules are complicated.

The problem is that life and death depends on shape, liberties, eyes, and connection all working together.

## The Relationship Between Eyes and Life

Eyes are empty points surrounded by your own stones.

They are important because they give a group internal breathing space.

If a group has no eyes, it may be captured once its outside liberties are removed.

If a group has one eye, it may still be in danger.

If a group has two true eyes, it is usually alive.

This is one of the most important ideas in [beginner Weiqi strategy](/articles/play-your-first-game).

## Why Two Eyes Usually Mean Life

A group with two separate true eyes usually cannot be captured.

Why?

Your opponent would need to fill both eyes.

But they cannot fill both at the same time.

If they try to play inside one eye, your group still has the other eye as a liberty.

If they try to fill the final eye, the move would usually be self-capture unless your group has already been captured legally.

That is why two true eyes normally make a group alive.

Beginners do not need to understand every exception yet.

Just remember:

Two separate true eyes are the basic shape of life.

## Why One Eye Is Not Enough

One eye can help, but one eye usually does not make a group alive.

If your group has only one eye and no way to make another, your opponent may eventually surround it.

Once the outside liberties are gone, the group may be captured.

This surprises many beginners.

They see one empty point inside their group and think:

"This group is safe."

But one eye is often only a start.

You usually need either a second eye, enough space to make a second eye, or a way to connect to safety.

## Simple Comparison

Here is a beginner-friendly way to think about common situations:

| Situation | Usually Alive? | Beginner Advice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Group with two true eyes | Yes | Usually safe. Do not waste extra moves defending it. |
| Group with one eye | Not yet | Look for a way to make a second eye or connect out. |
| Group with no eyes but many liberties | Maybe | Do not assume it is safe. Watch whether it can be surrounded. |
| Group connected to strong friendly stones | Often safer | Connection can help the group escape or share eye space. |
| Group cut off and surrounded | Usually in danger | Count liberties and look for eye-making moves quickly. |

This table is not a perfect rule.

Weiqi has many special shapes.

But it gives beginners a useful starting point.

## Why Connection Matters

Beginners often focus only on making eyes.

Eyes are important, but connection is also important.

If your weak group can connect to a stronger group, it may not need to make two eyes by itself.

Connected stones share liberties, eye space, and escape routes.

Disconnected stones may be attacked separately.

This is why beginners should focus on keeping groups connected.

A connected group is usually easier to protect than several small groups scattered across the board.

## When Sacrificing Stones Is Correct

Beginners often feel that every stone must be saved.

That is understandable.

Losing stones feels bad.

But in Weiqi, sacrificing a few stones can sometimes be correct.

If a small group is already weak and saving it would take many moves, you may be better off playing somewhere else.

Sometimes your opponent spends several moves capturing a few stones while you build territory, strengthen another group, or take a larger point.

This does not mean you should give up important groups easily.

It means you should ask:

"Is this group worth saving?"

If saving a small group costs too much, letting it go may be the stronger move.

## Common Mistakes That Kill Groups

### Waiting too long to make eyes

Beginners often ignore eye shape until the group is already surrounded.

By then, it may be too late.

### Getting cut apart

A group that gets separated into pieces may lose its ability to make two eyes.

### Running without a plan

Escaping toward the center can help, but running forever without making shape may only create a longer weak group.

### Defending the wrong point

Sometimes one move makes life, while another only delays capture.

This is why reading even one or two moves ahead matters.

### Assuming territory equals life

Territory and eyes are related, but they are not the same thing.

A group may surround some space and still not be fully alive.

If this idea feels confusing, review [territory in Weiqi](/articles/territory-in-weiqi).

## Five Practical Tips for Keeping Groups Alive

Here are five simple habits that will help your groups survive:

### 1. Count liberties when groups are attacked

If your group is under pressure, count its liberties before playing quickly.

This helps you see how urgent the situation is.

### 2. Look for two eye spaces

Ask whether your group can make two separate eyes.

If not, it may still be in danger.

### 3. Stay connected when possible

Connected stones are usually stronger than isolated stones.

Before playing elsewhere, check whether your weak stones can be cut apart.

### 4. Do not run forever

Running can help, but your group eventually needs shape, connection, or eyes.

Escaping without a plan can create more weakness.

### 5. Review groups you lost

After a game, look at the groups that died.

Ask:

"When did this group first become weak?"

This is one of the fastest ways to improve.

You can also review [common beginner mistakes](/articles/common-beginner-mistakes-in-weiqi) to spot habits that lead to dead groups.

## Should Beginners Study Life and Death?

Yes, but gently.

Life and death problems can help you recognize eye shape, liberties, and capture sequences.

But you do not need to solve difficult problems immediately.

Start with very simple shapes.

Ask:

- Can this group make two eyes?
- Can the opponent reduce it to one eye?
- Can it connect to another group?
- Does it have enough liberties to fight?

These questions are enough for the early stage.

## Key Takeaways

Before moving on, remember these ideas:

- An alive group is a group that cannot be captured with correct play.
- Two true eyes usually make a group alive.
- One eye is usually not enough.
- Connected groups are easier to keep alive.
- Sometimes sacrificing a small weak group is better than spending too many moves saving it.
- Beginners often lose groups by waiting too long, getting cut apart, or forgetting liberties.
- Life and death is not only about solving puzzles. It appears in every real game.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is an alive group in Weiqi?

An alive group is a group of stones that cannot be captured if both players play correctly.

### Do two eyes always mean a group is alive?

For beginners, two true eyes are usually enough to consider a group alive.

There are advanced exceptions and special shapes, but you do not need to worry about them at first.

One common exception beginners should learn gradually is the [false eye](/articles/what-is-a-false-eye-in-weiqi).

### Is one eye enough to live?

Usually, no.

A group with one eye often still needs a second eye, more space, or a connection to safety.

### Why do beginners lose groups suddenly?

Beginners often lose groups because they forget liberties, get cut apart, or wait too long to make eyes.

### Should I study life and death as a beginner?

Yes.

Start with simple life and death problems that show basic eyes, liberties, and captures.
