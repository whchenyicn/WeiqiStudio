Playing games is important.

But reviewing games is where many beginners improve fastest.

A review helps you understand what happened.

It shows you which mistakes repeated.

It gives your next game a clearer purpose.

You do not need to be strong to review your games.

You do not need professional analysis.

You only need a few simple questions and the patience to learn one thing at a time.

## Why Reviewing Games Helps You Improve Faster

When you play, you are busy making decisions.

You may not notice why a group became weak.

You may not notice when you missed an atari.

You may not notice that you fought too much and forgot territory.

After the game, the pressure is gone.

You can look calmly.

You can ask:

What happened?

That small pause turns experience into learning.

## You Do Not Need AI or Professional Analysis

AI can be useful later.

Professional review can be valuable.

But beginners do not need either one to benefit from review.

At the beginning, the biggest mistakes are usually simple:

- A group ran out of liberties
- A stone was left in atari
- A fight became too big
- Territory was ignored
- The same shape appeared again

You can learn from these without advanced tools.

A beginner review is not about finding the perfect move.

It is about finding one useful lesson.

## The Five Questions Every Beginner Should Ask

After each game, ask five questions:

1. Did I lose a group?
2. Did I miss an atari?
3. Did I build territory?
4. Did I fight unnecessarily?
5. What is one lesson for my next game?

These questions are enough.

They point your attention toward the most common beginner problems.

If you keep seeing the same mistakes, review [Common Beginner Mistakes in Weiqi](/articles/common-beginner-mistakes-in-weiqi).

## After Every Game Checklist

Use this checklist after each game:

After Every Game:

- Did I lose a group?
- Did I miss an atari?
- Did I build territory?
- Did I fight unnecessarily?
- What is one lesson for my next game?

Do not try to answer every question perfectly.

Just look honestly.

One clear answer is already useful.

## Identify One Mistake

Many beginners try to fix everything at once.

This quickly becomes overwhelming.

Instead, choose one mistake.

Maybe you lost a group because you forgot liberties.

Maybe you chased one stone for too long.

Maybe you defended a safe group and ignored the bigger board.

Choose one moment and ask:

What should I notice next time?

That is enough.

If you try to fix one mistake at a time, your games will slowly become clearer.

## Why Reviewing Immediately Helps

Reviewing soon after the game is useful because the game is still fresh.

You remember what you were thinking.

You remember which moves felt confusing.

You remember where you felt pressured.

This makes the review more honest.

If you wait too long, the game may become just a record of moves.

The feelings behind your mistakes are harder to remember.

You do not need a long review.

Even ten minutes can help.

## Simple 10-Minute Review Routine

Here is a simple routine:

1. Replay the game quickly.
2. Stop at the first moment that felt confusing.
3. Ask whether a group was weak.
4. Check whether anyone missed atari.
5. Look at territory on the board.
6. Choose one mistake.
7. Write one lesson for your next game.

This routine is short on purpose.

Beginners do not need to review every move.

You only need to learn one useful thing.

## How to Use Stronger Players' Feedback

Feedback from stronger players can be very helpful.

But it can also feel overwhelming.

A stronger player may see many mistakes that you cannot see yet.

That is normal.

When someone reviews your game, listen for one or two main ideas.

Ask simple questions:

- Which group was weakest?
- What was the biggest mistake?
- What should I practice next?

Do not worry if you do not understand everything.

Keep the advice that you can use in your next game.

## Common Review Mistakes Beginners Make

### Reviewing only wins

Wins can still contain many mistakes.

Review both wins and losses.

### Trying to fix every move

This is too much.

Choose one mistake and learn from it.

### Only checking the final score

The score matters, but the learning is in the decisions before the score.

Look for where the game changed.

### Blaming one bad move

Sometimes one move looks like the problem, but the real issue started earlier.

Maybe a group was weak for many moves before it died.

### Depending too much on AI

AI may show a better move, but beginners also need to understand why.

If the explanation is too advanced, keep the review simple.

### Not applying the lesson

A review is useful only if it changes your next game.

Choose one lesson and remember it before you play again.

## What Should You Review First?

Start with obvious things.

Look for:

- Dead groups
- Missed atari
- Unnecessary fights
- Empty corners
- Weak groups
- Large territories

Do not worry about advanced openings.

Do not worry about tiny point differences.

Beginner improvement comes from fixing big, clear mistakes.

## How Review Fits Into Beginner Study

Review should be part of your normal study routine.

You do not need to review for hours.

After each serious game, spend five to ten minutes looking back.

Then choose one small practice focus.

For example:

- Count liberties more carefully
- Protect weak groups earlier
- Stop chasing every stone
- Look at territory before fighting
- Slow down before answering

This keeps your study practical.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How should beginners review Weiqi games?

Beginners should replay the game, look for one clear mistake, and choose one lesson for the next game.

### Do I need AI to review Go games?

No.

AI can help later, but beginners can improve by asking simple questions about groups, atari, territory, and fighting.

### How long should a beginner review take?

Five to ten minutes is enough for many beginner games.

### Should I review every game?

Review serious games when you can.

Even a short review after most games will help.

### What if I cannot find my mistake?

Start with the simplest question:

Did I lose a group?

If you still feel unsure, ask a stronger player for one main suggestion.
