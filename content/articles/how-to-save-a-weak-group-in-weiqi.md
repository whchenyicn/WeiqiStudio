Every beginner creates weak groups.

This is normal.

You play a few stones.

Your opponent attacks.

Suddenly, the stones you thought were safe feel surrounded, cut off, or difficult to protect.

Learning how to save a weak group is one of the most useful beginner skills in Weiqi.

The goal is not to save every stone.

The goal is to recognize danger early and choose the simplest way to make your group safe.

## What Is a Weak Group?

A weak group is a group of stones that is not yet safe.

It may have too few liberties.

It may have no eyes.

It may be cut off from friendly stones.

It may be surrounded by your opponent.

It may be running through the center without a clear plan.

In simple terms:

A weak group is a group your opponent can attack.

If you are unsure whether a group is already safe, read [How to Know if a Group Is Alive in Weiqi](/articles/how-to-know-if-a-group-is-alive-in-weiqi).

## Why Beginners Create Weak Groups

Beginners often create weak groups because they focus on one move at a time.

They play a stone to attack.

Then another stone to chase.

Then another stone to make territory.

But the stones do not always work together.

Some stones become isolated.

Some stones get cut off.

Some stones are placed too deep inside the opponent's area.

This does not mean the moves were foolish.

It means the group needs attention.

## Warning Signs That a Group Is in Danger

A group may be weak if:

- It has very few liberties.
- It has no clear eye shape.
- It is cut off from your other stones.
- It is surrounded on several sides.
- It cannot easily connect to friendly stones.
- It is running without making shape.
- Your opponent can attack it while building territory.

You do not need to read deeply at first.

Just learn to notice these warning signs.

## Simple Comparison

Here is a beginner-friendly way to think about common weak-group situations:

| Situation | Good Response | Common Beginner Mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Group has few liberties | Extend, connect, or capture first | Playing elsewhere too quickly |
| Group has no eyes | Make eye shape or move toward safety | Assuming empty space is already an eye |
| Group is cut off | Connect to stronger stones if possible | Fighting alone with no support |
| Group is surrounded | Look for eyes, connection, or escape | Running without a plan |
| Group is too expensive to save | Consider sacrificing it | Spending many moves on stones that may still die |

This table is not a complete life-and-death guide.

It is a practical checklist for beginner games.

## Making Eyes Is Usually the Priority

If a group is trapped, making eyes is often the most important goal.

Eyes give a group internal breathing space.

A group with two real eyes is usually alive.

A group with no eyes may eventually be captured.

This is why beginners should learn to ask:

"Can this group make two eyes?"

If the answer is yes, look for the move that helps create them.

If the answer is no, you may need to connect, run, or sacrifice.

If eye shape still feels unclear, review [What Are Eyes in Weiqi?](/articles/eyes-in-weiqi) and [What Is a False Eye in Weiqi?](/articles/what-is-a-false-eye-in-weiqi).

## Connecting Is Often Better Than Fighting

When beginners are attacked, they often fight back immediately.

Sometimes that works.

But often, connecting to stronger stones is safer and simpler.

If your weak group can join another friendly group, it may gain liberties, eye space, and escape routes.

That can be much better than starting a new fight while your group is still weak.

Before attacking, ask:

"Can I connect this group to safety?"

If the connection is easy and valuable, it may be the best move.

## Running Toward the Center

Sometimes a weak group cannot make eyes immediately.

It may also be too far from friendly stones to connect.

In that case, running toward the center can help.

The center gives your stones more room.

It may create more liberties.

It may help your group move toward friendly stones.

It may also make your opponent spend moves chasing you.

But running is not enough by itself.

A group that runs forever without making shape can remain weak for a long time.

When you run, keep asking:

- Am I gaining liberties?
- Am I moving toward friendly stones?
- Am I creating eye space?
- Is my opponent becoming stronger while chasing me?

## When Sacrificing Stones Is Correct

Not every weak group should be saved.

This is hard for beginners.

It feels painful to give up stones.

But sometimes saving a weak group costs too many moves.

If the group is small, surrounded, and unlikely to make life, sacrificing it may be correct.

Your opponent may spend several moves capturing it.

During that time, you may play elsewhere and gain something larger.

This is closely related to understanding [when a group is dead in Weiqi](/articles/when-is-a-group-dead-in-weiqi).

The key question is:

"Is saving this group worth the cost?"

Sometimes the answer is no.

## Checklist Before Trying to Save a Group

Before spending several moves trying to save a weak group, ask:

1. How many liberties does this group have?
2. Can it make two real eyes?
3. Can it connect to stronger stones?
4. Can it run toward the center safely?
5. Is there an opponent cutting point I must protect?
6. Is the group important enough to save?
7. What do I gain if I sacrifice it instead?

You do not need perfect answers.

Even asking these questions will improve your decisions.

If liberties are still difficult, review [What Are Liberties in Weiqi?](/articles/liberties-in-weiqi).

## Five Practical Tips for Beginners

### 1. Notice weak groups early

Do not wait until your group is completely surrounded.

The earlier you notice weakness, the easier it is to fix.

### 2. Count liberties before panicking

If your group still has several liberties, you may have time.

If it has very few, the situation may be urgent.

### 3. Connect before fighting

If connecting to strong stones solves the problem, do that first.

Fighting with a weak group often creates more danger.

### 4. Make eyes when surrounded

If your group cannot escape, look for eye shape.

Two real eyes usually mean safety.

### 5. Let go when saving is too expensive

Sometimes the strongest move is to stop rescuing a doomed group.

Use your next move somewhere more valuable.

You can learn more habits like this in [Common Beginner Mistakes in Weiqi](/articles/common-beginner-mistakes-in-weiqi).

## Key Takeaways

Before moving on, remember these ideas:

- A weak group is a group that is not yet safe.
- Weak groups often lack liberties, eyes, connection, or room to escape.
- Making eyes is usually the priority when a group is trapped.
- Connecting to stronger stones is often better than fighting.
- Running toward the center can help, but only if it creates real safety.
- Sacrificing stones can be correct when saving them costs too much.
- Beginners should ask simple questions before trying to save every group.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a weak group in Weiqi?

A weak group is a group that can be attacked because it lacks safety, eyes, liberties, connection, or room to escape.

### How do I save a group in Go?

Start by checking liberties, then look for eyes, connection to friendly stones, or a safe way to run.

### Should I always save weak stones?

No.

Sometimes weak stones are too small or too expensive to save.

Sacrificing them may be better.

### Is running toward the center good?

Sometimes.

Running toward the center can create space and liberties, but it should lead toward safety.

### What should beginners check first?

Count liberties first.

Then ask whether the group can make eyes, connect, or escape.
