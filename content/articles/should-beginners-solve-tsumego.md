Many beginners hear that they should solve tsumego.

Then they open a problem and feel completely lost.

That is normal.

Tsumego can be very useful, but it needs to be practiced at the right level.

You do not need difficult problems.

You do not need to solve hundreds every day.

You only need simple problems that help you see liberties, captures, eyes, and weak groups more clearly.

## What Is Tsumego?

Tsumego means life-and-death problems.

In English, people often call them Go problems.

In Weiqi, tsumego usually asks a simple question:

Can this group live?

Can this group be captured?

Can you find the move that saves or kills a group?

The word may sound advanced, but beginner tsumego can be very simple.

It might involve one capture.

It might involve one eye shape.

It might involve counting liberties carefully.

## Why Tsumego Is Important

Tsumego trains reading.

Reading means looking ahead before you play.

You imagine:

- If I play here, what can my opponent do?
- If my opponent answers there, what happens next?
- Can this group make eyes?
- Can this stone be captured?

At first, you may only read one or two moves ahead.

That is enough.

Beginner tsumego helps you slow down and see small board situations clearly.

This supports real games because many beginner mistakes happen when players move too quickly.

## Should Beginners Start Immediately?

Yes, but gently.

Beginners can start solving very easy tsumego after learning:

- Liberties
- Capturing
- Atari
- Eyes

If you do not know what eyes are yet, read [What Are Eyes in Weiqi?](/articles/eyes-in-weiqi) first.

If you are still learning the basic flow of the game, play a few small games first with [How to Play Your First Game of Weiqi](/articles/play-your-first-game).

The goal is not to prove that you are clever.

The goal is to build simple habits.

## How Many Problems Should Beginners Solve Each Day?

Start small.

For many beginners, three to five easy problems per day is enough.

If that feels too much, do one problem.

If that feels easy, do a few more.

The number is less important than the quality of attention.

One problem solved carefully is better than twenty problems clicked through quickly.

## Quality Matters More Than Quantity

Many beginners rush through problems.

They guess.

They check the answer.

Then they move on.

This does not help much.

Before playing the answer, pause.

Try to understand:

- What is the threat?
- How many liberties does the group have?
- Where are the eyes?
- What is the opponent's best reply?

Even if you get the answer wrong, this thinking is valuable.

It trains your mind to look before playing.

## How Tsumego Improves Reading Ability

Tsumego gives you small, focused positions.

Instead of thinking about the whole board, you focus on one local situation.

This makes reading easier to practice.

Over time, you begin to recognize shapes faster.

You notice when a group has too few liberties.

You notice when an eye is false.

You notice when a group can be saved by connecting or capturing first.

This connects directly to [alive groups](/articles/how-to-know-if-a-group-is-alive-in-weiqi) and beginner life-and-death strategy.

## Study Activities Compared

Each study activity helps in a different way:

| Study Activity | Main Benefit |
|----------------|--------------|
| Playing games | Builds experience and confidence |
| Reviewing games | Helps you understand mistakes |
| Solving tsumego | Improves reading and life-and-death awareness |
| Reading books | Explains ideas and gives structure |

You do not need to choose only one.

The strongest beginner routine combines them gently.

For a broader plan, read [How to Study Weiqi Effectively as a Beginner](/articles/how-to-study-weiqi-effectively).

## Beginner Tsumego Routine

Here is a simple routine:

1. Choose very easy problems.
2. Solve three to five problems.
3. Think before checking the answer.
4. If you get one wrong, replay it slowly.
5. Stop before you feel tired or frustrated.

This routine can take five to ten minutes.

That is enough.

If you do it consistently, your reading will improve.

## Common Beginner Tsumego Mistakes

### Choosing problems that are too hard

Hard problems can be discouraging.

Start with problems that feel almost too easy.

### Guessing immediately

Do not click the first move that looks interesting.

Pause and read.

### Ignoring the opponent's best reply

Tsumego is not only about your move.

It is also about how your opponent answers.

### Doing too many problems at once

Too much practice can become tiring.

Short, regular practice is better.

### Treating tsumego as separate from games

Tsumego helps your real games.

Look for the same ideas when you play.

You may notice weak groups, false eyes, or capture chances more quickly.

If you keep repeating the same habits in games, review [Common Beginner Mistakes in Weiqi](/articles/common-beginner-mistakes-in-weiqi).

## How Tsumego Fits Into a Weekly Study Plan

Tsumego should be one part of your study.

It should not replace games.

It should not replace review.

A simple week might look like this:

- Play two or three small-board games.
- Review one mistake after each game.
- Solve easy tsumego two or three times.
- Read one short lesson.

This is enough for steady progress.

Do not make the routine so heavy that you stop enjoying the game.

## Key Takeaways

Before moving on, remember these ideas:

- Tsumego means life-and-death problems.
- Beginners should solve tsumego, but only at an easy level.
- Three to five simple problems per day is enough for many beginners.
- Quality matters more than quantity.
- Tsumego improves reading by teaching you to look ahead.
- Tsumego should support your games, reviews, and basic lessons.
- Stop before practice becomes frustrating.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should beginners solve tsumego?

Yes.

Beginners should solve easy tsumego to practice liberties, eyes, capturing, and simple reading.

### How many tsumego should I solve each day?

Start with three to five easy problems.

Even one careful problem is useful.

### Are tsumego the same as beginner Go problems?

Tsumego are life-and-death problems.

Many beginner Go problems are tsumego or closely related capture and survival problems.

### Should I solve hard tsumego?

Not yet.

Choose problems that help you build confidence and understand basic shapes.

### Can tsumego replace playing games?

No.

Tsumego is useful, but you still need real games and reviews to improve.
