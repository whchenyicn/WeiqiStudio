Territory is easy for beginners to understand.

You surround empty points.

Those points help you win.

Thickness is less obvious.

It may not give you points immediately.

But it can make the rest of your game easier.

Learning the difference between thickness and territory helps beginners understand why some strong moves look quiet at first.

## What Does Thickness Mean?

Thickness means strong stones that are difficult to attack.

A thick group is safe.

It has good shape.

It has enough liberties.

It is not easy for your opponent to cut, surround, or capture.

Because thick stones are strong, they can support future moves nearby.

They can help you attack.

They can help you build influence.

They can make your opponent's stones feel uncomfortable.

In simple terms:

Thickness is strength that can help later.

## Thickness Is Different From Territory

Territory is empty space you control.

Thickness is the strength of your stones.

Territory usually gives points more directly.

Thickness may not score immediately.

For example, a thick wall facing the center may not surround many points yet.

But later, that wall may help attack a weak group or build a large area.

If territory still feels new, review [What Is Territory in Weiqi?](/articles/territory-in-weiqi).

## Thickness Is Different From Influence

Thickness and influence are related, but they are not exactly the same.

Thickness is about the strength and safety of your stones.

Influence is about how those stones affect the board around them.

A thick group often creates influence.

But influence can be weak if the stones creating it are not actually safe.

This is why thickness comes first.

Strong stones create more reliable influence.

If you want the broader idea, read [What Is Influence in Weiqi?](/articles/what-is-influence-in-weiqi).

## Thickness vs Territory

Here is a beginner-friendly comparison:

| Thickness | Territory |
|-----------|-----------|
| Main purpose: create strong, safe stones | Main purpose: surround points |
| Immediate points: often unclear | Immediate points: easier to see |
| Long-term value: supports attacks, connections, and future territory | Long-term value: wins if it stays secure |
| Beginner advice: useful when it clearly helps future play | Beginner advice: learn to value secure points first |

Both matter.

You do not need to choose only one.

Good Weiqi balances solid points with strong positions.

## Why Beginners Overvalue Immediate Territory

Beginners often like moves that create visible points.

This makes sense.

The goal of the game is to score more territory.

But Weiqi is not only about the points you can see right now.

Sometimes a move that makes no obvious territory prevents your stones from becoming weak.

Sometimes it builds a strong wall.

Sometimes it makes your opponent's future moves harder.

If you only chase immediate territory, you may create thin, weak positions that can be attacked later.

Secure points are good.

But unsafe points can disappear.

## When Secure Territory Is Better

Territory is often better when the points are clear and safe.

Choose secure territory when:

- Your group is already strong.
- The territory is easy to defend.
- The move prevents an invasion.
- The game is near the end.
- You do not see a useful way to use thickness elsewhere.

Beginners should not feel guilty about taking territory.

Territory wins the game.

The problem is not territory.

The problem is chasing small territory while leaving larger problems behind.

## When Thickness Is More Valuable

Thickness may be more valuable when strength will help the rest of the board.

Building thickness can be useful when:

- Your stones were weak and need safety.
- Your opponent has weak stones nearby.
- The position faces a large open area.
- Your thick stones can support an attack.
- Your thick stones can help future connections.

Thickness is especially useful when it has a purpose.

Do not build thickness just because the word sounds strategic.

Ask:

What will these strong stones help me do later?

## Five Beginner Situations Where Thickness Is Useful

Here are five simple situations to notice:

1. You save a weak group and it becomes hard to attack.
2. You connect stones so they work together.
3. You build a wall facing a large open area.
4. Your strong stones make an opponent's weak group uncomfortable.
5. Your position becomes safe enough that you can [tenuki](/articles/when-should-you-tenuki-in-weiqi).

You do not need advanced judgment.

Just begin noticing when strong stones make future moves easier.

## Should Beginners Prioritize Thickness or Territory?

Beginners should learn territory first.

Territory is easier to see and count.

But beginners should also learn not to make weak shapes just to gain a few immediate points.

A simple rule is:

Take territory when it is secure.

Choose thickness when it keeps your stones safe or clearly helps future play.

If both choices are confusing, ask:

Which move leaves fewer weaknesses?

That question is often more useful than trying to calculate advanced strategy.

## Thickness and Opening Direction

Thickness often appears naturally from good opening direction.

Corners are efficient for territory.

Sides help expand.

The center is harder to turn into immediate territory, but strong stones facing the center may become useful later.

This connects to [where to play your first move](/articles/where-to-play-first-move-in-weiqi) and [why Weiqi players start in the corners](/articles/why-start-in-the-corners-in-weiqi).

## Thickness and Sente

Thick stones can make your forcing moves stronger.

If your opponent has weak stones near your thickness, your moves may become harder to ignore.

But do not chase sente blindly.

A small forcing move is still small.

If you are learning this timing, review [What Are Sente and Gote in Weiqi?](/articles/what-are-sente-and-gote-in-weiqi).

## Common Beginner Mistakes With Thickness

### Thinking thickness is territory

Thickness is useful, but it is not automatically points.

You still need to use it well.

### Building thickness with no purpose

Strong stones should help something.

If they do not attack, connect, expand, or support territory, they may be less valuable than they look.

### Ignoring real territory

Do not reject secure points just because thickness sounds deeper.

Beginners still need to learn how to win by territory.

### Making heavy groups

A group can be large without being thick.

If it is slow, surrounded, or difficult to save, it may become heavy instead of strong.

### Attacking too hard

Thickness can support attacks, but beginners should attack calmly.

The goal is often to build value while your opponent runs, not to capture everything.

## A Simple Way to Practice

After a game, find one strong group.

Ask:

Did this group help me later?

Then find one territory.

Ask:

Was this territory secure?

This simple review helps you see the difference between strength and points.

Over time, you will begin to notice when thickness matters.

## Key Takeaways

Before moving on, remember these ideas:

- Thickness means strong stones that are difficult to attack.
- Territory means empty points you control.
- Thickness is different from influence, though thick stones often create influence.
- Secure territory is valuable because it scores points.
- Thickness is valuable when it supports future play.
- Beginners often overvalue immediate territory and leave weak shapes behind.
- Choose thickness when it clearly makes your stones safer or your future moves stronger.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is thickness in Weiqi?

Thickness means strong stones that are difficult to attack and can support future play.

### What is thickness in Go?

Thickness in Go means the same thing as thickness in Weiqi.

It refers to strong, safe stones that can influence nearby areas.

### Is thickness the same as influence?

No.

Thickness is the strength of your stones.

Influence is how those stones affect the surrounding board.

### Is territory more important than thickness?

Territory wins the game, but thickness can help you create territory and attack later.

Both are useful.

### Should beginners study thickness early?

Beginners should learn territory first, then gradually notice how strong stones help future play.
