As you continue learning Weiqi, you will hear two important words:

Sente and gote.

These words can sound advanced at first.

But the basic idea is simple.

Sente means you play a move that your opponent feels they must answer.

Gote means you play a move that ends locally, allowing your opponent to play elsewhere next.

In beginner games, understanding sente and gote helps you see who has the initiative.

It also helps you choose moves more efficiently.

## What Does Sente Mean?

Sente means keeping the initiative.

If you play a move and your opponent needs to respond, your move is often called sente.

After your opponent responds, you may be able to play somewhere else first.

That is why sente is often described as keeping control of the flow of the game.

You create a situation.

Your opponent answers.

Then you still get to choose the next important area.

This does not mean your opponent is helpless.

It simply means your move was urgent enough that they did not want to ignore it.

## A Simple Sente Example

Imagine your opponent has a weak group with very few liberties.

You play a move that threatens to capture it.

Your opponent must save the group.

After they respond, you can turn your attention to another part of the board.

That kind of move may be sente.

The key idea is:

Your opponent needs to answer.

If they ignore the move, something bad may happen.

## What Does Gote Mean?

Gote means losing the initiative locally.

If you play a move and your opponent does not need to answer immediately, your move may be gote.

After you finish that move, your opponent can choose where to play next.

This can sound bad, but gote is not always a mistake.

Sometimes a gote move is large.

Sometimes it secures important territory.

Sometimes it saves a weak group.

Sometimes it is simply the correct move.

Gote only means you are not keeping the initiative in that moment.

It does not mean the move is wrong.

## A Simple Gote Example

Imagine you play a move that completes a small secure territory.

Your opponent does not need to respond nearby.

They can play on the other side of the board.

Your move may be gote.

But if that territory was important, the move may still be good.

This is why beginners should not think:

Sente is always good.

Gote is always bad.

That is too simple.

## Sente vs Gote

Here is a beginner-friendly comparison:

| Sente | Gote |
|-------|------|
| Definition: a move your opponent usually needs to answer | Definition: a move that lets your opponent play elsewhere next |
| Initiative: often keeps the initiative | Initiative: usually gives the next choice to your opponent |
| Typical Example: threatening to capture a weak group | Typical Example: calmly finishing a secure territory |
| Beginner Advice: useful, but do not chase it blindly | Beginner Advice: not bad if the move has enough value |

The important difference is not whether the move looks exciting.

The important question is:

Who gets to choose the next important move?

## Why Sente Matters

Sente matters because Weiqi is played across the whole board.

If you can make useful moves that your opponent must answer, you may get the first chance to play in several important areas.

This can help you:

- Attack weak groups.
- Build territory.
- Defend efficiently.
- Move from one important area to another.

Sente can make your play feel active and flexible.

But it is only useful when the move itself has value.

## Why Gote Is Not Necessarily Bad

Many beginners hear about sente and immediately want every move to be sente.

That is a trap.

Some positions need a calm gote move.

For example, if one of your groups is weak, saving it may be more important than playing a forcing move elsewhere.

If your opponent has a big weakness, attacking it may be sente.

But if your own group is about to die, defending may be the better move even if it is gote.

Good Weiqi is not about keeping sente forever.

It is about choosing valuable moves.

## How Sente Relates to Efficient Play

Efficient play means getting good value from each move.

A sente move can be efficient because it does two things:

- It helps your position.
- It makes your opponent respond.

This can let you gain value without spending too many extra moves.

But not every forcing move is efficient.

If you play a small sente move while ignoring a huge area of territory, you may still fall behind.

This connects to opening principles too.

When choosing early moves, it is often better to understand value, territory, and direction than to memorize patterns.

## Beginners Should Not Chase Sente at All Costs

Sente feels powerful.

It can feel like you are controlling the game.

But beginners sometimes chase sente too much.

They keep making small threats because they enjoy seeing the opponent answer.

Meanwhile, they may ignore large areas of the board.

They may forget to build territory.

They may leave their own groups weak.

That is not good play.

Before playing a forcing move, ask:

"Is this move actually valuable?"

If the answer is no, it may be better to play somewhere bigger.

## Three Practical Tips for Recognizing Sente

Here are three simple tips for beginner games:

### 1. Ask whether your opponent must answer

If your move creates a serious threat, your opponent may need to respond.

That is often a sign of sente.

### 2. Look for captures, weak groups, and urgent defenses

Moves that threaten capture or attack weak groups are often easier to recognize as sente.

If ignoring the move would cause real damage, the move may keep initiative.

### 3. Check whether the move is big enough

Not every forcing move is worth playing.

Even if your opponent answers, a tiny move may still be less important than a large move elsewhere.

Always compare the value of the move with the rest of the board.

## Sente, Gote, and Territory

Sente and gote are not separate from territory.

They are part of how players decide where to play.

Sometimes a sente move helps you build territory while your opponent answers.

Sometimes a gote move protects territory that is already valuable.

If you are still learning the goal of the game, review [What Is Territory in Weiqi?](/articles/territory-in-weiqi).

Territory gives your moves purpose.

Sente and gote help you understand timing.

## Sente in Your First Games

When you [play your first game](/articles/play-your-first-game), do not worry about labeling every move.

Just notice simple moments:

- Did my opponent have to answer?
- Did I get to play somewhere else next?
- Did I spend too many moves in one small area?

That is enough at first.

This also connects to [when you should tenuki in Weiqi](/articles/when-should-you-tenuki-in-weiqi), because tenuki depends on whether a local move truly needs an answer.

Over time, sente and gote will become easier to feel.

## Quick Recap

Before moving on, remember these key ideas:

- Sente means keeping the initiative.
- Gote means the opponent can usually play elsewhere next.
- Sente is useful, but only when the move has value.
- Gote is not automatically bad.
- Beginners should not chase sente at all costs.
- Efficient play means choosing moves that create real value.

The goal is not to keep Sente forever, but to choose moves that create the greatest value.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is sente in Weiqi?

Sente is a move that your opponent usually needs to answer, allowing you to keep the initiative.

### What is gote in Weiqi?

Gote is a move that usually lets your opponent choose the next important play elsewhere.

### Is sente always better than gote?

No.

Sente is useful, but a large gote move can be better than a small sente move.

### What is sente in Go?

Sente in Go means the same thing as sente in Weiqi.

It refers to keeping the initiative by playing moves your opponent needs to answer.

### Should beginners study sente and gote early?

Yes, but gently.

Beginners should understand the basic idea without trying to label every move perfectly.
