Eyes help groups stay alive in Weiqi.

But not every eye-shaped space is a real eye.

Some spaces look safe at first, but can still be destroyed or filled by your opponent.

These are called false eyes.

False eyes are one reason beginners lose groups that seemed safe.

The good news is that you do not need advanced life-and-death reading to understand the basic idea.

You only need to learn the difference between a real eye and a false eye.

## What Is a False Eye?

A false eye is an empty point that looks like an eye but does not truly keep a group alive.

At first glance, it may seem surrounded by your stones.

But because your stones are not fully connected, or because your opponent can attack a key point, the eye is not actually secure.

In simple terms:

A false eye looks safe, but it can fail.

This is why beginners should be careful when counting eyes.

If you need the basic definition first, read [What Are Eyes in Weiqi?](/articles/eyes-in-weiqi).

## Why Beginners Mistake False Eyes for Real Eyes

Beginners often look only at the empty space.

They see one surrounded point and think:

"That is an eye."

But an eye is not only about empty space.

It also depends on the stones around that space.

If the surrounding stones can be cut, captured, or forced apart, the eye may be false.

This is why false eyes are confusing at first.

They look similar to real eyes, but they do not protect the group in the same way.

## Real Eye vs False Eye

Here is a simple comparison:

| Real Eye | False Eye |
|----------|-----------|
| Definition: an empty point securely surrounded by connected stones | Definition: an empty point that looks surrounded but is not fully secure |
| Keeps group alive? Usually helps, especially as part of two true eyes | Keeps group alive? Not reliably |
| Can opponent fill? Usually not directly without self-capture | Can opponent fill? Often yes after cutting, capturing, or reducing the shape |
| Beginner tip: check whether the surrounding stones are connected | Beginner tip: do not count it as safe until the shape is secure |

This table is only a starting point.

But it captures the most important beginner idea:

A real eye is secure.

A false eye is not.

## Why Two False Eyes Do Not Necessarily Make Life

You may have learned that two eyes usually make a group alive.

That is true when the eyes are real eyes.

But two false eyes do not necessarily make a group alive.

If both eyes can be destroyed, filled, or reduced by your opponent, the group may still die.

This is one of the most common beginner misunderstandings in life and death.

The goal is not simply to find two empty spaces.

The goal is to make two secure eyes.

## Common Situations Where False Eyes Occur

False eyes often appear when stones are almost connected, but not quite.

They can also appear when a group is being squeezed by the opponent.

Here are a few common beginner situations:

### The surrounding stones are cut

If the stones around the eye can be separated, the eye may not be real.

Your opponent may attack the cutting point and destroy the shape.

### One of the surrounding stones can be captured

If an important stone around the eye has too few liberties, your opponent may capture it.

Once that stone is gone, the eye disappears.

This is why [liberties](/articles/liberties-in-weiqi) matter so much.

### The group has no room to make a second eye

Sometimes a group has one eye-shaped space, but no way to make another.

Even if the first eye is useful, the group may still be in danger.

### The eye shape depends on a weak connection

If your group is held together by a weak connection, your opponent may cut it apart.

Once the group is split, the eye shape may collapse.

## How to Identify a False Eye During a Game

You do not need to read deeply at first.

Start with simple questions:

- Are the stones around the eye connected?
- Can one of those stones be captured?
- Can my opponent cut the group apart?
- Does the group have another real eye?
- If my opponent plays nearby, does the eye disappear?

If the answer to any of these questions is worrying, the eye may be false.

Do not panic.

Just treat the group as not fully safe yet.

## A Simple Beginner Example

Imagine your group surrounds one empty point.

It looks like an eye.

But one of the stones beside that empty point is in atari.

If your opponent captures that stone, the empty point is no longer surrounded.

That means the eye was not secure.

It was a false eye.

The lesson is simple:

Do not only look at the empty point.

Look at the stones around it.

## How Beginners Can Avoid False Eye Mistakes

The best way to avoid false eye mistakes is to slow down when a group is under attack.

Before deciding that your group is alive, check the shape carefully.

Ask:

- Does this group have two separate eyes?
- Are both eyes real?
- Are the surrounding stones connected?
- Does my opponent have an obvious cutting move?
- Does any important stone have too few liberties?

You do not need to answer perfectly.

Even asking these questions will help you avoid many beginner mistakes.

## False Eyes and Your First Games

When you [play your first game](/articles/play-your-first-game), you do not need to identify every false eye perfectly.

Just remember that not every eye-shaped space is safe.

If your group has only one eye-shaped point, keep looking for:

- A second eye
- A connection to another group
- More liberties
- A way to escape

That is enough for the beginner stage.

## Key Takeaways

Before moving on, remember these ideas:

- A false eye looks like an eye but does not truly keep a group safe.
- A real eye is surrounded by secure, connected stones.
- Beginners often mistake false eyes for real eyes because they look only at the empty point.
- Two false eyes do not necessarily make a group alive.
- False eyes often happen when stones can be cut, captured, or separated.
- To avoid mistakes, check the stones around the eye, not only the empty space.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a false eye in Weiqi?

A false eye is an eye-shaped empty point that does not actually make a group safe because the surrounding stones are not secure.

### Is a false eye the same in Go?

Yes.

Weiqi and Go are different names for the same game, so a false eye in Go works the same way as a false eye in Weiqi.

### Can two false eyes make a group alive?

Not necessarily.

A group needs real, secure eyes to be safely alive.

### How can beginners recognize false eyes?

Check whether the stones around the eye are connected, whether any can be captured, and whether your opponent can cut the shape apart.

### Should beginners study false eyes deeply?

Not at first.

Beginners should understand the basic warning: not every eye-shaped space is a real eye.
