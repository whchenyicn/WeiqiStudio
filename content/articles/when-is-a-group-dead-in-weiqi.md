One of the hardest beginner moments in Weiqi is realizing that a group cannot be saved.

The stones are still on the board.

They may still have liberties.

They may even look like they have space.

But if they cannot make life, they may already be dead.

This can feel strange at first.

In Weiqi, a group does not need to be physically removed from the board before players understand that it is lost.

Learning when a group is dead helps you stop wasting moves and start playing more efficiently.

## What Is a Dead Group?

A dead group is a group of stones that cannot avoid being captured if both players play correctly.

It may still have liberties.

It may still be surrounded by some empty space.

It may still look like it has a chance.

But if it cannot make two real eyes, connect to safety, or escape, it is usually dead.

This is part of life and death in Weiqi.

If you need the opposite idea first, read [How to Know if a Group Is Alive in Weiqi](/articles/how-to-know-if-a-group-is-alive-in-weiqi).

## Why a Group Can Look Alive but Still Be Dead

Beginners often judge groups by size.

If a group has many stones, it looks strong.

If it surrounds some empty space, it looks safe.

If it still has liberties, it looks alive.

But size alone does not make a group live.

A group can look large and still be cut off.

It can surround space that is not enough to make two eyes.

It can have eye-shaped points that are actually [false eyes](/articles/what-is-a-false-eye-in-weiqi).

This is why beginners sometimes lose groups unexpectedly.

The outside shape looked safe, but the inside shape could not survive.

## Liberties, Eyes, and Survival

Liberties are the empty points directly next to a stone or group.

As long as a group has liberties, it remains on the board.

But liberties alone do not always mean the group is safe.

If your group has no way to make eyes, your opponent may eventually fill the outside liberties and capture it.

Eyes are different.

Eyes are internal spaces that help a group keep permanent breathing room.

This is why [eyes](/articles/eyes-in-weiqi) matter so much.

In simple beginner terms:

- Liberties keep stones on the board right now.
- Eyes help groups survive long term.

If liberties still feel unclear, review [liberties](/articles/liberties-in-weiqi).

## Why Two Real Eyes Usually Mean Life

A group with two real eyes usually cannot be captured.

Your opponent cannot fill both eyes in a normal way because the final move would be self-capture.

That means the group has permanent internal liberties.

This is why players often say:

Two eyes live.

But the word real is important.

Two false eyes do not necessarily make life.

A group needs secure eyes, not just empty spaces that look like eyes.

## Alive Group vs Dead Group

Here is a beginner-friendly comparison:

| Alive Group | Dead Group |
|-------------|------------|
| Can survive? Yes, with correct play | Can survive? Usually no, if the opponent plays correctly |
| Has enough eyes? Usually has two real eyes or can make them | Has enough eyes? Cannot make enough real eyes |
| Should you continue fighting? Usually no need to defend extra | Should you continue fighting? Only if there is a realistic way to escape or make life |
| Beginner advice: do not waste moves defending already alive stones | Beginner advice: do not spend many moves saving stones that cannot live |

This table is a guide, not a perfect rule.

Some positions are complicated.

But beginners do not need advanced reading yet.

Start by asking whether the group can make real eyes, connect, or escape.

## Why Some Groups Are Impossible to Save

Some groups are too surrounded.

They have no room to make two eyes.

They cannot connect to friendly stones.

They cannot escape into open space.

In those situations, playing more moves inside the group may only give your opponent more points or more strength.

This is painful to accept at first.

But it is also freeing.

Once you realize a group is dead, you can stop spending moves there and play somewhere more valuable.

## When Beginners Should Stop Trying to Rescue Every Stone

Beginners often try to save every stone.

This is natural.

Every stone feels important because you placed it yourself.

But Weiqi is not about saving every stone.

It is about creating the best overall position.

Sometimes a few stones are not worth saving.

If saving them takes many moves and still does not work, you may be falling behind elsewhere.

Before trying to rescue a weak group, ask:

- Can this group make two real eyes?
- Can it connect to another group?
- Can it escape into open space?
- Is saving it worth more than playing somewhere else?

If the answer is no, it may be time to let the stones go.

## Why Sacrificing Stones Can Be Stronger

Sacrificing stones means giving up some stones on purpose to gain something more valuable.

This sounds strange to beginners.

But it is an important part of Weiqi.

If your opponent spends several moves capturing a small dead group, you may use that time to build territory elsewhere.

You may strengthen another group.

You may take a larger point.

You may reduce your opponent's territory.

The sacrifice is only bad if you gain nothing in return.

If giving up stones helps the rest of your board, it can be the stronger move.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

### Trying to save every stone

Not every stone can or should be saved.

Sometimes letting go is correct.

### Counting liberties but ignoring eyes

A group may have liberties now, but still die later if it cannot make eyes.

### Trusting false eyes

False eyes can make a dead group look alive.

Check whether the eye shape is actually secure.

### Running without making shape

Escaping is useful only if your group can eventually connect, make eyes, or reach safety.

Running forever can create a longer weak group.

### Playing inside a dead group too long

If a group cannot live, extra moves inside it may be wasted.

Look for bigger moves elsewhere.

You can review more patterns in [Common Beginner Mistakes in Weiqi](/articles/common-beginner-mistakes-in-weiqi).

## Simple Beginner Examples

Imagine a group surrounded on the outside with only one small eye.

If it has no room to make a second eye and cannot connect out, it is probably dead.

Now imagine another group with two separate real eyes.

Even if surrounded, it is usually alive.

Finally, imagine a small group that can run toward friendly stones.

That group may not be alive yet, but it may still have a chance.

These examples are simple, but they give you the right beginner questions:

- Can it make two real eyes?
- Can it connect?
- Can it escape?
- Is it worth saving?

## Key Takeaways

Before moving on, remember these ideas:

- A dead group is a group that cannot avoid capture with correct play.
- A group can look alive but still be dead if it cannot make real eyes.
- Liberties keep stones on the board now, but eyes help groups survive long term.
- Two real eyes usually mean a group cannot be killed.
- Some groups are impossible or too expensive to save.
- Sacrificing stones can be correct if you gain value elsewhere.
- Beginners should learn when to defend, when to escape, and when to let stones go.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a dead group in Weiqi?

A dead group is a group that cannot avoid being captured if both players play correctly.

### Are dead stones the same in Go?

Yes.

Weiqi and Go are different names for the same game, so dead stones in Go work the same way as dead stones in Weiqi.

### Can a group with liberties still be dead?

Yes.

A group may still have liberties but be unable to make eyes, connect, or escape.

### Should beginners always save weak groups?

No.

Sometimes saving a weak group costs too many moves.

In those cases, playing elsewhere may be better.

### How do I know if a group is dead?

Start by asking whether it can make two real eyes, connect to friendly stones, or escape.

If none of those are possible, the group may be dead.
