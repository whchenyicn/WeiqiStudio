When you watch a Weiqi game begin, you will often notice something interesting.

Players usually start near the corners.

They do not begin by filling the center.

They do not usually play directly on the edge.

Instead, the first few moves often appear near the four corners of the board.

This is not an accident.

Corners are the easiest places to make territory.

That simple idea explains many opening moves in Weiqi.

If you are still wondering where to play first, start with the guide to [where to play your first move in Weiqi](/articles/where-to-play-first-move-in-weiqi).

## The Basic Principle

A useful beginner principle is:

Corners → Sides → Center.

This means:

- Corners are usually easiest to make territory.
- Sides are usually next.
- The center is usually hardest to secure early.

This is not a strict rule.

It is a guideline.

But it is a very helpful guideline when the board feels too open.

## Why Corners Are Easiest

Weiqi is about surrounding territory.

Territory is empty space that only one player can safely control.

In the corner, the board itself helps you.

Two edges are already there.

That means you need fewer stones to surround an area.

Imagine building a fence around a small garden.

If two sides of the garden already have walls, you only need to build the other sides.

That is what a corner does in Weiqi.

The edge of the board acts like a wall.

This makes corners efficient.

If you need a refresher, read [What Is Territory in Weiqi?](/articles/territory-in-weiqi).

## Why Sides Are Next

The sides are also useful, but they are not as efficient as corners.

On the side, only one edge helps you.

You still have one natural boundary, but you need more stones to surround territory than you would in the corner.

This is why players often begin in the corners and then expand along the sides.

The side gives you room to grow.

It can connect your corner stones to larger areas.

It can also help you build territory while still influencing the center.

For beginners, this is a good pattern to remember:

Start near a corner, then look for useful moves along the side.

## Why the Center Comes Later

The center is the most open part of the board.

It has no edge helping you.

To surround territory in the center, you usually need more stones and stronger shape.

That makes the center harder to control early in the game.

This does not mean the center is bad.

The center can become very powerful later.

Stones in the center can connect groups, attack weak stones, and influence many areas at once.

But for complete beginners, starting only in the center can feel confusing because it is hard to turn center influence into clear territory.

If you want to understand this idea more clearly, read [What Is Influence in Weiqi?](/articles/what-is-influence-in-weiqi).

That is why the center often becomes more important after corners and sides have developed.

## Simple Comparison

Here is a beginner-friendly way to compare the three areas:

| Area | Difficulty | Territory Efficiency | Typical Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Corner | Easiest | Highest | Early game |
| Side | Medium | Good | Early to middle game |
| Center | Hardest | Lower at first | Middle to later game |

This table is not a mathematical rule.

It is a practical learning shortcut.

Corners usually give beginners the clearest results.

Sides help expand those results.

The center becomes important once you understand how stones work together.

## A Simple Example

Imagine you want to surround a small area.

In the corner, two board edges already help you.

On the side, one board edge helps you.

In the center, no board edge helps you.

So the center usually requires more stones to surround the same kind of space.

That is why beginners often find corner territory easier to understand.

It is not because corners are magical.

It is because they are efficient.

## Is This Always True?

No.

Corners → Sides → Center is a guideline, not an absolute rule.

Professionals sometimes play unusual openings.

They may play higher moves.

They may emphasize influence.

They may choose strategies that are difficult for beginners to understand at first.

That is normal.

Professional Weiqi includes many different styles.

But beginner Weiqi should begin with clear principles.

Corners are easier to understand, so they are a good place to start.

## Do Not Memorize Too Much

It is tempting to memorize opening moves.

You may see professional games and think:

"If I copy this, I will play well."

But memorizing without understanding can become a problem.

If your opponent plays a different move, you may not know what to do.

Instead, focus on the reason behind the moves:

- Corners are efficient.
- Sides expand territory.
- Center influence becomes useful later.
- Stones should work together.

This kind of understanding will help you more than copying openings too early.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

### Playing only in the center

The center looks important, but it is hard to surround early.

Beginners who start only in the center may struggle to make secure territory.

### Ignoring the corners

If you ignore the corners completely, your opponent may take easy territory while you chase unclear goals elsewhere.

### Thinking the guideline is a law

Corners are important, but Weiqi is flexible.

Sometimes fighting, defending, or responding to your opponent matters more than following a simple rule.

## How This Helps Your First Games

When you [play your first game](/articles/play-your-first-game), you do not need an advanced opening plan.

Just remember:

Start near a corner.

Look for ways to expand along the side.

Do not panic if the center looks empty.

That is enough to begin.

As you play more games, you will gradually understand when the center becomes important.

## Five Practical Beginner Takeaways

Before moving on, remember these five ideas:

1. Corners are usually easiest because two board edges help you.
2. Sides are useful because one edge helps you build territory.
3. The center is powerful later, but harder to secure early.
4. Corners → Sides → Center is a guideline, not a law.
5. Understanding opening principles is better than memorizing openings too early.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do Weiqi players start in the corners?

Because corners are usually the easiest places to make efficient territory.

The board edges help reduce the number of stones needed to surround space.

### Should beginners always play in the corner first?

Usually, starting near a corner is a good idea.

But it is not an absolute rule.

### Is the center bad in Weiqi?

No.

The center can become very powerful, especially later in the game.

It is simply harder for beginners to use well at the start.

### What does Corners → Sides → Center mean?

It means players often begin by taking corners, then expand along the sides, and use the center more as the game develops.

### Is this the same in Go?

Yes.

Weiqi and Go are different names for the same game, so the same beginner opening principle applies.
